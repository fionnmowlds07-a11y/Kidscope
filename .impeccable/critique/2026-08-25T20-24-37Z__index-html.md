## Overall Impression

A small, honestly-built site with real craft in the places that are easy to skip on a volunteer/nonprofit budget — documented contrast ratios that are actually followed, thorough keyboard/focus handling, and a search feature with real content awareness. The biggest opportunity: the homepage treats its five destinations and its emotional centerpiece (the hero photo) as equally-weighted, undifferentiated content, when the actual visitor is usually a specific, somewhat anxious person with one specific need (how do I get referred / where do I call).

## What's Working

1. Verified accessibility discipline — contrast ratios are computed and commented inline (--ink: 13.83:1), and grepping confirmed the low-contrast --brand blue is never used as text color anywhere in the stylesheet. A rule that was actually followed sitewide, not just where convenient.
2. Honest edge-case handling — the Springboard partner tile has no confirmed URL, so it ships as a plain, non-interactive tile rather than guessing a link. Most sites skip this.
3. Keyboard/focus completeness — nav, search, and the accessibility panel all implement Escape-to-close-with-focus-return and outside-click-to-close, layered on top of native <details> semantics.

## Priority Issues

**[P1] The "How can we help?" grid treats all 5 destinations as equally important.**
- Why it matters: A referred parent needs "Being referred" or "Contact us" urgently; "Research and publications" (a UCC academic page) is not urgent for almost anyone. Equal visual weight forces everyone to read all five, and is the direct cause of two separate cognitive-load-checklist failures (chunking and minimal-choices).
- Fix: Visually promote 1-2 cards for referral/contact (size, accent, or a "Start here" label); consider moving "Research and publications" out of the primary grid entirely.
- Suggested command: /impeccable layout

**[P2] The full 6-page nav is hidden behind "Menu" at every viewport, including desktop.**
- Why it matters: .site-nav is position: absolute inside a <details> with no breakpoint that ever reveals it inline — confirmed by reading the only nav-adjacent media query, which just reflows the header grid. This is the classic "Hidden Navigation" anti-pattern; the homepage's card grid partly papers over it, but what-we-do.html/contact.html show only 2 cards each, so the gap is worse elsewhere on the site.
- Fix: Add a persistent inline nav bar above a wide breakpoint (~900-1000px), keeping the hamburger for narrow viewports only.
- Suggested command: /impeccable layout

**[P2] The walk-in-appointments warning is styled at two different severities on two different pages.**
- Why it matters: Same sentence, soft blue .callout on the homepage vs. amber .notice warning box (the CSS itself calls .notice "the one emphasised warning in the brief") on contact.html. The design system disagrees with its own stated intent — a direct Consistency violation on an operationally important fact.
- Fix: Pick one severity deliberately and apply it everywhere the message appears.
- Suggested command: /impeccable harden

**[P2] The reassuring hero photo is ordered after all hero text on mobile.**
- Why it matters: Below 860px the layout goes single-column with DOM order hero-copy -> hero-media, so the one image doing emotional work (a clinician letting a child listen to a teddy bear's heartbeat) is pushed below the heading, lede, consultant line, and four badges — for the audience most likely to be on a phone.
- Fix: Reorder for mobile so the image appears in or near the first viewport.
- Suggested command: /impeccable adapt

**[P3] Hero badges restate the lede paragraph almost word-for-word.**
- Why it matters: "Established 2010," "Ages 0-6 years," "Free of charge," and "Cork city northwest" are all already in the sentence directly above them — pure repetition, no new information.
- Fix: Drop the badges, or make them carry facts not already in the lede (e.g. "Referral required," "No cost to families").
- Suggested command: /impeccable distill

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan (Confused First-Timer):** "Multi-stakeholder community paediatric clinic" is unexplained jargon in sentence one. The 5-card grid gives no signal about which card is theirs — "Being referred" and "Research and publications" look identical in weight. The "Please note" callout states what's not allowed (walk-ins) without linking to what is the path (ask your PHN). To find a phone number, Jordan must scroll past hero -> 5 cards -> callout -> 6 partner logos to reach the footer.

**Sam (Accessibility-Dependent User):** Confirmed strength — uniform :focus-visible (including a white-on-dark variant for the hero/footer), and the low-contrast brand blue is never used as text. Red flag: partner logos open in a new tab with only a visually-hidden warning — a low-vision sighted user at 200% zoom gets no visual cue before leaving the site. Gap: #search-results has role="listbox" but no aria-live, so a screen-reader user isn't told how many results appeared as they type.

**Casey (Distracted Mobile User):** The one warm/reassuring image on the page is very likely off-screen on first load (see P2 above). No persistent "call the clinic" affordance in the thumb zone — the phone number lives only in the footer. The header search bar drops to its own full-width row on mobile, pushing real content down further before Casey sees anything.

## Minor Observations

- Search has a silent no-op: pressing Enter with zero results resolves to undefined (harmless since the "no matches" text is already visible, but the keypress itself is unacknowledged).
- Search isn't wrapped in a <form> — if JS fails to load, typing + Enter does nothing, with no <noscript> fallback pointing to the Menu button.
- Image filenames contain literal spaces (home photo.jpg, hse logo.jpg) — works today, but a known source of breakage on some static hosts.
- The walk-in warning appears a third time, verbatim, in every page's footer — likely intentional emphasis, but reads as nagging within one visit.
- No Open Graph/Twitter Card tags — a shared homepage link (text/email/WhatsApp, plausible for how parents actually pass this URL around) gets no title/image preview.
- Sitewide (not on index.html itself, so out of scope for this run's score): 42 em-dashes used as a repeated separator in resources.html link labels (detector-confirmed, advisory-only), and two more side-tab-pattern borders on what-we-do.html's .aims list and the .notice box on contact.html/referrals.html — worth a look next time those pages are critiqued.

## Questions to Consider
